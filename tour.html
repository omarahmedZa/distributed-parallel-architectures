How to use this course

Course tour

Learn how the lessons are organized, how to read the diagrams and worked examples, and how to get the most from the research activities.

1. Start with Fundamentals

Open Fundamentals first. It introduces parallelism basics, memory models, cache/memory hierarchy intuition, MPI/OpenMP concepts, and speedup/efficiency calculations. It explicitly connects forward to key lectures (memory hierarchy → Lecture 2, MPI/OpenMP → Lecture 5, speedup/efficiency → Lecture 9).

2. Follow the module progression

Use the course map to move through Modules 1–5 in order. Each lecture builds on earlier concepts and links back to prerequisites where helpful. Use the pager at the bottom of each lesson to go forward/backward.

3. How each lecture is structured

  • Learning objectives: what you should be able to explain or analyze after the lesson.
  • Core ideas and mechanisms: in-depth explanations of architectures, protocols, and techniques.
  • Design decisions & trade-offs: why architects choose one approach over another and what is gained/lost.
  • Limitations & scalability: bottlenecks, overheads, and scaling behavior.
  • Research problems: open challenges and active directions.
  • Diagrams, tables, and worked examples: visual explanations and step-by-step calculations (including speedup/efficiency where relevant).
  • Recap & check-for-understanding: quick prompts to test your grasp before moving on.
  • Research Activities: two-paper literature review, architecture comparison, and a real-world case study for deeper, evidence-based study.

4. Using visuals and interactions

Figures and tables are designed to make mechanisms concrete (e.g. hierarchies, topologies, protocol states, timelines, scaling behavior). Where interactive/animated elements are provided, use controls to explore parameter changes and observe effects; the surrounding text explains the key takeaway.

5. Research activities

Every lecture ends with a dedicated Research Activities section. Read the cited papers and case-study materials critically: compare claims, methods, results, and limitations. Treat citations and links as starting points for verification and deeper literature review.

Ready? Return to the course map or begin with Fundamentals.
